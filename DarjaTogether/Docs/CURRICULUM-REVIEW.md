# Curriculum review

This is an original, family-led starter curriculum for complete beginners, using simple picture-supported activities, optional speaking and gradual Arabic-script noticing. It aims at Central Algerian / Algiers Darja. It is not a native-speaker-certified course or a formal proficiency assessment.

## Included content

- 6 stages: First Words, Little Listener, Little Speaker, Darja Explorer, Story Teller, Family Conversationalist.
- 36 distinct weekly units and 180 short sessions, intended for 5–8 minutes each.
- 180 vocabulary or useful-chunk cards, each with Arabic, Latin pronunciation, English, French, an emoji and relevant usage notes.
- 5 new cards per week: the first session introduces 3, the second adds 2, and the remaining sessions reuse them. A week can take as long as the child needs.
- 36 model phrases in both translation languages, 36 family activities and 36 optional script prompts.
- 12 original mini stories with 73 illustrated-by-emoji lines, plus a comprehension question and answer for each story.
- 11 pronunciation tips, 16 parent-guidance notes and 7 source records in the app.

Feminine self-description is used in the example dialogue. Requests that address a girl or woman include masculine counterparts in card notes. First-person action forms apply to both girls and boys. Story stretch phrases are supported listening exposure, not extra memorisation requirements.

## Dialect and sound

Darja spelling and family vocabulary vary. Notes call out alternatives for such words as cat, grandmother, bowl, rain and yes, as well as q/g variation. The reading aid uses ḥ, ʿ, kh, gh, q, sh and dj; it is not formal IPA. White and eggs intentionally share the spelling بيض but have distinct vowel guides, pictures and a usage note.

A fluent family member's recording is the preferred sound model. Standard Arabic device speech may pronounce Darja unnaturally and must remain labelled as approximate. No native voice recordings, external course audio or claims of pronunciation scoring are supplied by this curriculum.

## Reference checks and attribution

Selected common forms were checked against an Algiers teacher's public first-person teaching material: [Learn with Aymen](https://learnwithaymen.com/), [greetings](https://learnwithaymen.com/phrases/greetings), [adjectives](https://learnwithaymen.com/phrases/describing-things), [verbs](https://learnwithaymen.com/phrases/verbs), [food](https://learnwithaymen.com/phrases/food-and-drink), and [spelling conventions](https://learnwithaymen.com/spelling.php). The [Institut du monde arabe language centre](https://www.imarabe.org/fr/centre-langue-et-civilisation-arabes-clca) provides institutional context for teaching dialectal and Standard Arabic as distinct offerings.

These are reference checks, not endorsement or exhaustive validation of every entry. Stories, activities, progression and translations were independently authored. No external paid lessons or recordings are redistributed. A fluent Algiers-speaking family member should review the family's preferred forms when recording them.

## Validation

Automated integrity checks verify all 180 card IDs are unique, each card belongs to exactly one week, all references resolve, every weekly unit has 5 sessions, no session has more than 5 offered cards, review references point to earlier weeks, and required Arabic/English/French fields are present. Editorial review aligned every review activity with its actual offered cards and checked feminine forms, source scope and the distinction between participation and mastery.
